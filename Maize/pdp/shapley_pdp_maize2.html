Reading the figure

One panel per predictor. Each point is one explained year: across, the predictor’s own value that year, measured against the same month a year earlier; up, the Shapley value the model gave it. The line is a smooth fitted through those points.

Height is the size of the contribution, in percentage points of the forecast price change. A point sitting at +8 means the predictor added eight points to that year’s forecast, and the distance from zero is what makes one predictor weigh more than another.

Slope is how that contribution changes as the predictor itself moves. Flat means the size of the movement makes no difference. Steepening means it is non-linear, so a small shortfall and a large one do not count in proportion. Where the line crosses zero the predictor stops pushing the forecast one way and begins pushing it the other.

Panels are ordered by Relative Intensity, shown at the top right of each one, and how many are drawn is set on the left.
