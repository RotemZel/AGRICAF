Reading the figure

Each row is a predictor and each column a forecast month, with one panel per forecast horizon. Reading a row across shows how a predictor’s role changes through the year.

Relative Intensity is the share of the forecast’s total explanatory magnitude that a predictor accounts for, pooled over every explained year: the size of its Shapley value divided by the size of all of them added up. It runs from 0 to 1 and sums to 1 down a column, so a single dark cell implies the rest of that column is pale and the forecast rests on a narrow base.

Impact keeps the direction as well as the size. It is positive where the predictor’s own value and its Shapley value share a sign, negative where they differ, and the scale is centred on zero. Each cell is the mean over the explained years.

These values record what the forecast leaned on. They do not identify cause, and where two predictors move together the method may credit either one.
