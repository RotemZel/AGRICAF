Reading the figure

Each box is the spread across the explained years, so this view answers whether a predictor mattered consistently or only in particular years.

Intensity plots each year on its own terms: the size of that year’s Shapley value divided by the size of every predictor’s that year. A tight box means the predictor carried a similar share every year; a long box means its role was episodic.

Impact plots the same years with their direction kept, positive where the predictor’s value and its Shapley value share a sign. A box straddling zero marks a relationship that changed direction from one year to the next.

The box spans the middle half of the years, the line inside it is the median, and the whiskers reach the furthest year within 1.5 times that span. Points are the individual years. Fill colour is the predictor’s value pooled over all of them, so it matches the same cell on the global view.
